Who They Were

R.J. — Ricky James III

"My Hero, My Little Brother, My Everything"

R.J. was six years younger than me, but he was always my hero. Even though I was the older brother, he was the one I could count on for anything — and I mean anything. It didn't matter what it was, he showed up. Every. Single. Time.

I was adopted into his family before he was even born. But when he came into this world, something clicked. We just connected. He became my dude, my ride-or-die. We worked together, hung out together, partied together, pushed boundaries we probably shouldn't have... but man, there was never a dull moment when we got together.

R.J. had his life together. Two motorcycles. A truck. Graduated early. Already a foreman at his construction job. He was the fucking man. About a month before he passed, he got a tattoo that nobody knew about — a bleeding red rose, placed right over his heart. That rose now lives forever in our logo, dripping purple — a symbol of the love that never fades.

R.J. was riding his motorcycle, coming home from a drive, when a car came too wide off the freeway and hit him. He died on impact. I wasn't ready. None of us were. He was the best man at my second wedding. He was supposed to be here forever.

"Even though he was six years younger, he will always be my hero."

T-Mainney — Todd

"My Blood, My Brother, My Best Friend"

T-Mainney was my biological brother. We were split apart when I was 4 and he was 2½. Our biological parents couldn't take care of us five kids. One night, they gathered us in the living room, told us they loved us, that they always would... but they could no longer take care of us. Next thing we knew, we were being ripped apart from our parents in the blink of an eye.

The trauma of that night... watching our dad beat on our mom, being in a drive-by shooting at just 3 years old, then being separated from everything we knew. It was devastating. But I was blessed — I was adopted into a family I'm proud to call mine.

When I was 19 and Todd was almost 18, we reconnected. And it was like we were never split. We became best buds right from the jump. When we hung out — bowling, dancing, partying, whatever — we could feed off each other's energy, killing everything we touched. In a good way. We worked jobs together. He was the best man at my first wedding.

Todd was working three jobs. Had his own car. Loved getting tattoos. Always wore his dog tags. He enjoyed life... but he couldn't overcome the trauma from his past. The mental battles he fought were ones nobody else could see. One day, at just 22 years old, he took his own life.

"Those dog tags he always wore now hang in our logo — forever reminding us he's still here."

The Three of Us

"One Adopted, One Blood, All Brothers"

What made it even more special was that R.J. and T-Mainney — one my adopted brother, one my blood brother — they connected too. The three of us together? Unstoppable. Those were the most real, blessed moments of my life. Losing one brother was hard enough. Losing both of them... it broke me for a long time.

I blamed my addictions and actions on losing them. I used their deaths as an excuse to be a fuck-up, to burn down everything I touched, to watch my whole world turn to ash. I lost everything. I couldn't take care of my kids. I was completely alone with nowhere to turn.

But one day I realized — I couldn't keep blaming my brothers for my choices. I needed to take accountability. I was afraid that if I let go, they'd become faded memories that would eventually disappear. Then I started having the same dream, over and over. I never dream. I never remember dreams. But this one kept coming back. That's when I knew — they weren't asking me to hold on to pain. They were asking me to carry them forward.

"They couldn't become faded memories. So I made them live forever."
